# Best Gazpacho

> [!info]+ At a Glance
> **Active:** 20 min · **Chill:** 6+ hrs (or overnight) · **Total:** ~6.5 hrs
> **Serves:** 8–12 (about 1 quart) · **Difficulty:** easy · **Source:** [NYT Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1017577-best-gazpacho)

> [!note]- Notes
> - Seville-style: no bread, creamy orange-pink (not lipstick red) — the generous olive oil emulsifies with tomato and cucumber juice into a smooth, almost fluffy texture.
> - More of a drink than a soup — serve in frosted glasses or chilled tumblers.
> - Must chill at least 6 hours; adjust salt and vinegar again before serving.

## Ingredients

#ingredients
- [ ] About 2 lbs ripe red tomatoes, cored and roughly chunked
- [ ] 1 Italian frying (cubanelle) pepper or other long light-green pepper (e.g. Anaheim), cored, seeded, chunked
- [ ] 1 cucumber, about 8 inches, peeled and chunked
- [ ] 1 small mild onion (white or red), peeled and chunked
- [ ] 1 clove garlic
- [ ] 2 tsp sherry vinegar, more to taste
- [ ] Salt
- [ ] 1/2 cup extra-virgin olive oil, more to taste, plus more for drizzling

## Directions

1. Combine tomatoes, pepper, cucumber, onion and garlic in a blender (or deep bowl for a hand blender), in batches if needed. Blend at high speed until very smooth, at least 2 minutes, scraping down the sides occasionally.
2. With the motor running, add the vinegar and 2 teaspoons salt. Slowly drizzle in the olive oil — the mixture will turn bright orange or dark pink and emulsify like a salad dressing. If it seems watery, drizzle in more oil until creamy.
3. Strain through a strainer or food mill, pushing all the liquid through; discard solids. Transfer to a large (preferably glass) pitcher and chill until very cold, at least 6 hours or overnight.
4. Before serving, adjust salt and vinegar. If very thick, stir in a few tablespoons ice water. Serve in glasses, over ice if desired, or in a bowl, with a few drops of olive oil on top.

## To Serve

- Ice-cold, in glasses, olive oil drops on top — peak too-hot-to-eat weather food.

## Variations & Substitutions

- The olive oil quantity is the difference between "delicious" and "take-it-or-leave-it" — don't skimp.
