# Cheesy Hasselback Potato Gratin

> [!info]+ At a Glance
> **Prep:** 30 min (mostly slicing) · **Bake:** 90 min · **Total:** ~2 hrs
> **Serves:** 6 · **Difficulty:** medium · **Source:** [NYT Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1017724-cheesy-hasselback-potato-gratin)

> [!note]- Notes
> - Potato gratin × Hasselback mash-up from "The Food Lab" — slices stand **vertically** on edge, so every bite gets creamy interior and crisped ridge.
> - A mandoline or food processor makes the slicing sane.
> - Buy a few extra potatoes: they must be packed tightly and standing straight. Longer, thinner russets fill the dish better; you may not need all the cream.

## Ingredients

#ingredients
- [ ] 3 oz finely grated Gruyère or Comté
- [ ] 2 oz finely grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
- [ ] 2 cups heavy cream
- [ ] 2 medium garlic cloves, minced
- [ ] 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves, roughly chopped
- [ ] Kosher salt and black pepper
- [ ] 4 to 4 1/2 lbs russet potatoes, peeled and sliced 1/8-inch thick (7–8 medium; buy extra)
- [ ] 2 tbsp unsalted butter

## Directions

1. Adjust oven rack to middle position and heat oven to 400°F. Combine the cheeses in a large bowl; transfer 1/3 of the mixture to a separate bowl and set aside. Add cream, garlic and thyme to the larger portion and season generously with salt and pepper. Add potato slices and toss with your hands until every slice is coated, separating any that stick together so cream gets between them.
2. Grease a 2-quart casserole dish with the butter. Pick up a handful of potatoes, organize into a neat stack, and set in the dish with edges aligned vertically. Continue around the perimeter and into the center until all potatoes are added — very tightly packed. Slice and coat an extra potato if needed to fill.
3. Pour the excess cream mixture over the potatoes until it comes halfway up the sides of the dish. You may not need all of it.
4. Cover tightly with foil and bake 30 minutes. Remove foil and bake until pale golden brown, about 30 minutes more. Sprinkle with the reserved cheese and bake until deep golden brown and crisp on top, about 30 minutes longer. Rest a few minutes before serving.

## To Serve

- Showpiece side for roasts and holidays; rest briefly so the cream sets.

## Variations & Substitutions

- Comté for Gruyère works identically; any good alpine melter will do.
