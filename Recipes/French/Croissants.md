# Croissants

> [!info]+ At a Glance
> **Total:** 24 hrs, largely unattended (~2 hrs active) · **Makes:** 8 croissants
> **Difficulty:** hard — the project of projects · **Source:** [NYT Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1022053-croissants) · [Claire's step-by-step video](https://youtu.be/vpwY3nmLLaA)

> [!note]- Notes
> - Claire Saffitz's full roadmap to bakery-quality lamination. Butter temperature and rolling pressure get easier with experience — follow the script exactly on attempt one.
> - Use European or European-style butter (higher fat, more pliable).
> - Timeline anchors: T-24h start dough → T-18h laminate → overnight chill → T-4.5h shape and proof → bake.
> - The same laminated dough makes pain au chocolat or ham-and-cheese croissants (roll to 15×16 instead).
> - Best within 1–2 hours of baking; revive at 350°F for 5–8 minutes.

## Ingredients

#ingredients
**Détrempe (dough)**
- [ ] 4 2/3 cups / 605 g all-purpose or bread flour, plus more for dusting
- [ ] 1/3 cup / 66 g granulated sugar
- [ ] 1 tbsp plus 1/2 tsp / 12 g kosher salt
- [ ] 2 1/4 tsp / 7 g active dry yeast
- [ ] 3/4 cup plus 2 tbsp / 214 g water, at room temperature
- [ ] 1/2 cup / 120 g whole milk, at room temperature
- [ ] 1/4 cup / 57 g unsalted butter, cut into 1/2-inch pieces, chilled

**Butter block & assembly**
- [ ] 1 1/2 cups / 340 g unsalted European-style butter (3 sticks), chilled
- [ ] All-purpose flour, for rolling
- [ ] 1 large egg yolk
- [ ] 1 tbsp heavy cream

## Directions

**Day 1 — dough & butter block (T-24h)**

1. In a stand mixer with the dough hook, stir together flour, sugar, salt and yeast. Make a well, pour in water and milk, and mix on low until a tight, smooth dough forms around the hook, ~5 minutes. Cover with a damp towel; rest 10 minutes.
2. On medium-low, add the chilled butter pieces all at once and mix, scraping down occasionally, until the dough is very smooth, stretchy, and not at all sticky, 8–10 minutes.
3. Ball the dough, place seam-down on a floured surface, and cut a deep "+" into the top (helps it rise square). Return slashed-side up to the bowl, cover, and rise at room temp until 1½× its size, 45–60 minutes. Refrigerate 4–12 hours.
4. Butter block: lay the 3 sticks side by side on parchment, fold into a packet, and beat flat with a rolling pin into a scant 1/2-inch layer, fusing the sticks. Re-wrap with neat right-angle folds into an 8-inch square, then roll to fill the packet completely, forcing out air — a level, straight-edged square. Refrigerate.

**Day 1 evening — lamination (T-18h)**

5. Deflate the doubled dough with your heel. Using the four slash points, stretch into a rough square no more than 8 inches per side.
6. Wrap in two crossed sheets of plastic and roll to fill the plastic as an 8-inch square with sharp corners. Freeze 20 minutes.
7. Roll the dough on a floured surface to 16×8 inches (barely wider than the butter block). Brush off all flour.
8. Match firmness: dough slightly colder than butter; the butter should bend without breaking (rest it a few minutes if brittle). Invert the butter block onto the center of the dough, sides parallel; peel off the parchment.
9. Fold both overhanging dough flaps over the butter to meet in the middle (stretch as needed) and pinch all seams shut — no butter peeking. Flour underneath and rotate 90° so the seam runs vertical.
10. Beat along the surface with the pin to lengthen, then roll along the seam into a 24-inch-long, 1/4-inch-thick slab. Push the dough away/toward you rather than pressing down; keep sides straight and check for sticking.
11. Trim the short ends square where the butter doesn't extend. Pierce any air bubbles and continue.
12. **Double turn:** fold each short end to the midline, leaving an 1/8-inch gap where they meet, then fold the whole slab in half along that gap — a four-layer "book."
13. Wrap tightly; if thicker than 1 1/2 inches, roll to flatten. Freeze 15 minutes, then refrigerate 1 hour.
14. Rest 5 minutes at room temp, then roll again into a long 3/8-inch-thick slab.
15. **Simple turn:** fold in thirds like a letter. Wrap, freeze 15 minutes, refrigerate 1 hour.
16. Roll to a 14×17-inch slab (it will fight back — get close). Brush off flour, wrap, and slide onto a baking sheet. Freeze 20 minutes, then chill overnight (8–12 hours).

**Day 2 — shape, proof, bake (T-4.5h)**

17. Set racks in upper and lower thirds. Put a skillet of simmering water on the oven floor and close the door — a steamy proof box.
18. Line two sheet pans. Roll the dough back to 17×14 if needed, dust off *all* flour, trim the short sides square to exactly 16 inches, and cut into four 4×14-inch rectangles.
19. Cut each rectangle corner-to-corner into two long triangles (8 total). Trim each base at a slight angle so the long sides are equal.
20. For each: tug the base corners out to ~3 inches wide, then gently stretch the lower half to the tip. Roll up snugly from the base, point centered, light pressure — don't stretch tight. Rest each crescent on its point, 4 per pan. Cover very loosely with plastic. (If dough softens, freeze a few minutes.)
21. The oven should be humid but not hot (70–75°F — hotter melts the butter). Proof the covered pans inside until doubled, extremely puffy, and delicately jiggly, 2–2 1/2 hours. Don't poke; don't rush — underproofed croissants bake up dense.
22. Refrigerate the pans 20 minutes while removing the skillet and heating the oven to 375°F.
23. Whisk yolk and cream; gently brush only the smooth surfaces, avoiding the exposed cut layers.
24. Bake 20 minutes, rotate and swap racks, and bake until deeply browned, another 10–15 minutes. Cool completely on the pans.

## To Serve

- Within an hour or two of baking, with good jam and better coffee.

## Variations & Substitutions

- Same dough → pain au chocolat or ham-and-cheese (roll to 15×16 in step 16 and shape accordingly).
