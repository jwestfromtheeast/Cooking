# San Francisco-Style Vietnamese American Garlic Noodles

> [!info]+ At a Glance
> **Prep:** 5 min · **Cook:** 10 min · **Total:** 15 min
> **Serves:** 4 · **Difficulty:** easy · **Source:** [NYT Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1023012-san-francisco-style-vietnamese-american-garlic-noodles)

> [!note]- Notes
> - Based on Helene An's original at San Francisco's Thanh Long restaurant; adapted from "The Wok."
> - Yes, 20 cloves of garlic. Yes, Parmesan with fish sauce. Trust it.
> - Cooking the spaghetti in shallow water concentrates the starch for a creamier emulsion.

## Ingredients

#ingredients
- [ ] 4 tbsp unsalted butter
- [ ] 20 medium garlic cloves, minced or smashed in a mortar and pestle
- [ ] 4 tsp oyster sauce
- [ ] 2 tsp light soy sauce or shoyu
- [ ] 2 tsp fish sauce
- [ ] 1 lb dry spaghetti
- [ ] 1 oz grated Parmesan or Pecorino Romano (heaping 1/4 cup)
- [ ] A small handful of thinly sliced scallions (optional)

## Directions

1. Melt the butter in a wok or saucepan over medium heat. Add the garlic and cook, stirring, until fragrant but not browned, about 2 minutes. Add the oyster sauce, soy sauce and fish sauce, and stir to combine. Remove from the heat.
2. Meanwhile, bring 1 1/2 inches of water to a boil in a 12-inch skillet or sauté pan over high heat (or just enough water to cover the spaghetti in a Dutch oven). Add the pasta, stir a few times so it doesn't clump, and cook, stirring occasionally, until just shy of al dente — about 2 minutes short of the package time.
3. Using tongs, transfer the pasta to the garlic sauce along with whatever water clings to it (reserve the pasta water). Increase the heat to high, add the cheese, and stir and toss vigorously until the sauce is creamy and emulsified, about 30 seconds. Too watery? Keep reducing. Greasy? Splash in more pasta water and re-emulsify. Stir in the scallions and serve immediately.

## To Serve

- Excellent alongside seafood — or with things stirred in (see below).

## Variations & Substitutions

- Add raw shell-on shrimp with the garlic at the start; or fold in tarako/mentaiko, tobiko, ikura, crab, lobster, or even caviar.
