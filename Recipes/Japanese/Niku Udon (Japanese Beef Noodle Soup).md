# Niku Udon (Japanese Beef Noodle Soup)

> [!info]+ At a Glance
> **Prep:** 10 min · **Cook:** 20 min · **Total:** 30 min
> **Serves:** 4 · **Difficulty:** easy · **Source:** [NYT Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1023569-niku-udon-japanese-beef-noodle-soup)

> [!note]- Notes
> - Shortcut: instant dashi powder (Hondashi) instead of homemade.
> - Shaved beef for shabu-shabu/sukiyaki is at most Asian markets; cheesesteak-quality shaved beef works too, and plain ground beef gives different-but-delicious results.
> - Boil udon in **unsalted** water — the noodles are already salty.
> - Gyudon variation below.

## Ingredients

#ingredients
**Dashi**
- [ ] 20 g / about 2 (4-inch) squares kombu
- [ ] 20 g / about 2 cups loosely packed katsuobushi (dried bonito flakes)

**Beef**
- [ ] 1 lb thinly shaved beef
- [ ] 1 small yellow onion, thinly sliced
- [ ] 1 bunch scallions — whites in 2-inch segments halved lengthwise, light greens thinly sliced at a sharp angle
- [ ] 1 tbsp granulated sugar
- [ ] 1/4 cup sake
- [ ] 2 tbsp soy sauce
- [ ] 2 tbsp mirin

**Serving**
- [ ] 2 tbsp soy sauce, plus more as needed
- [ ] 3 tbsp mirin, plus more as needed
- [ ] 1 lb fresh or frozen udon noodles
- [ ] Shichimi or nanami togarashi (optional)

## Directions

1. **Dashi:** Combine 2 quarts cold water and the kombu in a medium saucepan over medium heat. As soon as the water boils, remove from heat and add the katsuobushi, gently pushing it under (don't stir vigorously). Steep 5 to 10 minutes. Strain, discard the solids (or reserve for a weaker second batch), and return the dashi to the pan. Keep warm but don't boil.
2. **Beef:** Combine the shaved beef, onion, scallion whites, sugar, sake, soy sauce, mirin and 1 cup of the dashi in a large skillet. Bring to a simmer over high heat, stirring. Keep cooking over high, stirring frequently, until the beef and onions are tender and the mixture is moist but not soupy, 10 to 15 minutes.
3. Meanwhile, bring a pot of unsalted water to a boil and cook the udon per package directions.
4. Add the serving soy sauce and mirin to the reserved dashi, stirring. Taste and adjust — the broth should balance savory and sweet.
5. Divide noodles across 4 large bowls. Ladle broth over, then divide the beef mixture (with its pan juices) across the bowls. Top with scallion greens and serve immediately with shichimi togarashi.

## To Serve

- Big bowls, broth first over noodles, beef piled on top, scallion greens and togarashi to finish.

## Variations & Substitutions

- **Gyudon:** skip noodles and broth (make only enough dashi to cook the beef); serve the beef over steamed rice with beni shoga and a poached or fried egg.
