# Marcella Hazan's Bolognese Sauce

> [!info]+ At a Glance
> **Prep:** 15 min · **Simmer:** 3+ hrs (lazy, mostly unattended) · **Total:** 4+ hrs
> **Makes:** 2 heaping cups — 6 servings, dresses ~1½ lbs pasta · **Difficulty:** easy · **Source:** [NYT Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1015181-marcella-hazans-bolognese-sauce)

> [!note]- Notes
> - "The gold standard" — reader-crowned after Hazan's death in 2013. Milk before wine is the signature move: it tenderizes and mellows the meat.
> - The simmer should be the *laziest* possible — an intermittent bubble. Add 1/2 cup water whenever it dries out; at the end no water remains and the fat separates (stir it back in).
> - Freezes beautifully; make a double batch.

## Ingredients

#ingredients
- [ ] 1 tbsp vegetable oil
- [ ] 3 tbsp butter, plus 1 tbsp for tossing the pasta
- [ ] 1/2 cup chopped onion
- [ ] 2/3 cup chopped celery
- [ ] 2/3 cup chopped carrot
- [ ] 3/4 lb ground beef chuck (or 1 part pork to 2 parts beef)
- [ ] Salt and freshly ground black pepper
- [ ] 1 cup whole milk
- [ ] Whole nutmeg
- [ ] 1 cup dry white wine
- [ ] 1 1/2 cups canned imported Italian plum tomatoes, cut up, with juice
- [ ] 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 lbs pasta
- [ ] Freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano, at the table

## Directions

1. Put the oil, butter and onion in a pot over medium heat. Cook, stirring, until translucent, then add the celery and carrot and cook ~2 minutes, coating them well.
2. Add the beef, a large pinch of salt and a few grindings of pepper. Crumble with a fork, stir, and cook just until it loses its raw red color.
3. Add the milk and simmer gently, stirring frequently, until it has bubbled away completely. Grate in about 1/8 teaspoon of nutmeg and stir.
4. Add the wine and simmer until evaporated, then add the tomatoes and stir well. When they begin to bubble, turn the heat down to the *laziest* of simmers — an intermittent bubble. Cook uncovered 3 hours or more, stirring occasionally, adding 1/2 cup water whenever needed to prevent sticking. At the end, no water should remain and the fat should separate; stir it into the sauce, then taste for salt.
5. Toss with cooked, drained pasta and the extra tablespoon of butter. Serve with Parmigiano-Reggiano at the table.

## To Serve

- Traditionally tagliatelle; also spectacular in [[The Big Lasagna]] in place of (or alongside) the tomato sauce layer.

## Variations & Substitutions

- Part pork deepens the flavor; never skip the milk step.
