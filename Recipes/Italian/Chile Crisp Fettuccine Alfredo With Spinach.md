# Chile Crisp Fettuccine Alfredo With Spinach

> [!info]+ At a Glance
> **Prep:** 5 min · **Cook:** 20 min · **Total:** 25 min
> **Serves:** 6 · **Difficulty:** easy · **Source:** [NYT Cooking](https://cooking.nytimes.com/recipes/1023609-chile-crisp-fettuccine-alfredo-with-spinach)

> [!note]- Notes
> - Italian-Chinese mash-up: chile crisp sizzled in butter, tempered by cream — restaurant-complex flavor with zero chopping.
> - Add more of the chile crisp *solids* than oil for the most flavor; brands vary in heat, so adjust.
> - Pregrated Parmesan works fine here.

## Ingredients

#ingredients
- [ ] Salt
- [ ] 4 tbsp butter
- [ ] 1–2 tbsp chile crisp, plus more to taste
- [ ] 1 cup heavy cream
- [ ] 1 lb dried fettuccine
- [ ] 1 (5-oz) package baby spinach
- [ ] 3/4 cup finely grated Parmesan (2 1/4 oz), plus more for serving

## Directions

1. Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. While it heats, melt the butter with the chile crisp in a very large skillet or Dutch oven over low heat.
2. Whisk in the cream and keep warm over low — it should steam, not bubble.
3. Cook the fettuccine until al dente per package directions. Use tongs to transfer the noodles into the cream mixture, reserving the pasta water.
4. Add the spinach and turn with tongs until the noodles are well coated.
5. Add the Parmesan and toss, still over low heat, until the noodles are slicked with creamy sauce, loosening with a spoonful or two of pasta water if needed. Top with more Parmesan and chile crisp; serve immediately.

## To Serve

- Extra Parmesan and a spoonful of chile crisp on top.

## Variations & Substitutions

- Store-bought or homemade chile crisp both work — a future [[Chile Crisp]] component, perhaps.
